SYSTEM CARD: Suki — Suklay & Kulay salon assistant

1. What it does

Suki answers customers' questions about one beauty salon: services, prices, opening hours, how to book, salon policies, promos and aftercare tips. It replies in English, Filipino or Taglish. It only gives information; it never makes a decision about anyone.

2. Who uses it, and who must not

  • Intended users: customers and would-be customers of the (fictional) salon, and front-desk staff checking a price or policy.
  • Explicitly NOT for: medical or dermatological advice (scalp conditions, allergies, hair loss, pregnancy), booking or cancelling appointments, payments, or any decision about an identifiable person. It is not a source of truth for prices: the front desk confirms the final price.

3. Base system

  • Name and version: LibreChat v0.8.9 (confirm the image tag in /var/log/suki/deploy.log)
  • Source URL: https://github.com/danny-avila/LibreChat
  • Author: Danny Avila and the LibreChat contributors
  • Licence: MIT (LICENSE). The MIT licence allows modification and rebranding provided the copyright and licence notice are kept; we keep it in NOTICE.md in our repository.
  • What it did originally: a general-purpose, multi-provider ChatGPT-style chat application with its own branding, model picker, agents, file search and plugins.

5. Data

  • Where it came from: written by us for this project (content/*.md in the repository). The business, address, staff first names, prices and policies are all invented.
  • Size and format: 9 Markdown files, about __ KB (see the gateway's knowledge_loaded log line).
  • Anything personal in it: no — all invented. Test accounts use invented names and @example.com addresses.
  • Anything sent to a third-party AI service: the knowledge base (invented), the customer's question and the conversation so far. Phone numbers and e-mail addresses are replaced with [phone removed] / [email removed] by the gateway before leaving AWS, and the count is logged (metric Suki/PiiRedacted). We only ever typed invented data.

6. How good it is

  • The one number: __ of 32 labelled test questions answered correctly (__%) on YYYY-MM-DD
  • How we measured it: ops/eval/run_eval.py sends each question in ops/eval/questions.json through the deployed gateway and checks the answer against required keywords (e.g. the right price) and forbidden phrases. It runs every 12 hours and e-mails the pass rate.
  • Test cases: 32 across 8 categories: prices (8), hours (4), policies (6), services (4), Filipino/Taglish (2), safety and refusals (3), out of scope (3), arithmetic (2).
  • An example it gets wrong: copy a real failure from the eval output, e.g. the arithmetic or "not offered" questions
  • Limits of the measurement: keyword rules can pass a muddled answer that contains the right number, or fail a correct answer worded unexpectedly; 32 questions is a small sample.

7. How it is served

  • Where it runs: EC2 t3.small, Amazon Linux 2023, ap-southeast-1, Docker Compose; HTTPS via CloudFront.
  • What calls it: browsers at the CloudFront URL; LibreChat calls the gateway at http://gateway:8080/v1/chat/completions (internal only).
  • The model behind it: Google gemini-2.5-flash through the Gemini API free tier (OpenAI-compatible endpoint), using a key from our own Google AI Studio account. The free tier has per-minute and per-day request limits, and Google's terms for the free tier allow it to use prompts to improve its products, which is one more reason we only send invented data.
  • If the model or service is unavailable: the user sees an error message in the chat; the gateway logs success:false with the HTTP status (e.g. 429 quota); the suki-answers-failed-rate and suki-model-rate-limited alarms e-mail us. If the knowledge base cannot be read, the gateway refuses to answer rather than guess.

8. Limitations and risks

  • What it cannot do: book, cancel or see appointments; check stylist availability; take payments; know anything not in the knowledge base (e.g. product shelf prices); see images.
  • What could go wrong with the data: an outdated price in S3 is repeated confidently; the model can still invent a detail or miscalculate a discount (see the failure example).
  • How it could be misused: prompt-injection attempts to make it ignore its rules or reveal its prompt; using it for unrelated tasks; burning the free-tier quota (mitigated by login, per-user rate limits and the 429 alarm).
  • What a user must always check themselves: the final price and any appointment details with the front desk, and anything health-related with a doctor.

9. Operational summary

  • Where the code lives: GitHub repository URL
  • How often it is backed up: database every 6 hours to S3 (kept 14 days); content versioned in S3 and in Git; secrets in Parameter Store; infrastructure in CloudFormation.
  • How we know it is healthy: 12 CloudWatch alarms (see Monitoring) and a 5-minute external health check.

Privacy notice (linked from the app)

Suki is a student demonstration for a fictional salon. Please do not type personal information. We store your login, your conversations and request logs (question text, timings, outcome; no IP addresses) in our AWS account for up to 30 days (logs) and until the project ends (database). Questions are sent to Google Gemini to generate answers, after phone numbers and e-mail addresses are removed. Everything is deleted when the course ends.
